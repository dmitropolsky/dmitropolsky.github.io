Syllabus

Overview

This course is a a graduate level introduction to natural language processing (NLP), the study of human language from a computational and statistical perspective. The course will provide a solid background in the mathematical and algorithmic foundations of NLP, programming experience in building NLP models. The course will enable students to read NLP papers.

The first half of the semester will mostly be on classical methods and models: language modeling, Markov and n-gram models, hidden Markov models, tagging, log-linear models, probibalistic context-free grammars and parsing. The second half of the semester will concern neural and modern methods: neural networks, computational graphs, attention and transformers, reinforcement learning methods.

Prerequisites

The most important prerequisites are mathematical maturity, and experience in programming in Python. Theoretical assignments and exams will include proofs, so if you have taken something like algorithms, discrete math, or a similar course, you should be well prepared. You will also be very well prepared if you have taken a general machine learning course.

However, there are no strict prerequisities. You can look at the notes under "References" on the previous course webpage linked under "Textbook" below to get a sense of the course content and mathematical level. If you still have doubts, please talk to the course instructor.

Course structure

The course will consist of lectures (attendance required), and:

  • Two midterms exams (20% each)
  • Final exam (30%)
  • ~ 4 problem sets (20%)

Please note that this is subject to change, especially in the beginning of the semester.
